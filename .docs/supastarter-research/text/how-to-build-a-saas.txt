How to Build a SaaS Application in 2026
Building a SaaS (Software as a Service) product is one of the most popular business models in tech. But where do you start? This guide walks you through everything you need — from choosing your tech stack to launching your product.
Step 1: Validate Your Idea
Before writing any code, validate that people will pay for your product:
Talk to potential customers
— find 10 people with the problem you're solving
Research competitors
— understand what exists and where the gaps are
Build a landing page
— test interest with a waitlist or pre-launch page
Define your MVP
— the smallest product that solves the core problem
Step 2: Choose Your Tech Stack
A modern SaaS tech stack in 2026 typically includes:
Frontend/Fullstack Framework
Next.js
(React) — most popular, largest ecosystem
Nuxt
(Vue) — great DX, strong for internationalization
Database
PostgreSQL
— the standard for SaaS (hosted on Supabase, Neon, or self-managed)
ORM
— Prisma (beginner-friendly) or Drizzle (performance-focused)
Authentication
better-auth
— modern, open-source, supports 2FA, passkeys, and RBAC
Payments
Stripe
— most flexible, best for larger businesses
Lemon Squeezy
— simplest, handles tax compliance as merchant of record
Styling
Tailwind CSS
— utility-first CSS for rapid UI development
shadcn/ui
— accessible, composable component library

Authentication & User Management

Frequently Asked Questions
How long does it take to build a SaaS?
With a boilerplate like supastarter, you can have a working MVP in
days to weeks
instead of months. The actual timeline depends on the complexity of your unique features.
How much does it cost to build a SaaS?
Development costs vary widely. Using a boilerplate ($200-400) plus hosting ($0-50/month with free tiers) means you can launch for under $500.
Do I need to know how to code to build a SaaS?
While no-code tools exist, building a production SaaS typically requires programming knowledge. However, AI coding agents (Cursor, Claude Code) make it much more accessible.
